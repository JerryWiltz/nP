Watkins-Johnson cascadable amplifier examples

From Application Information for Thin Film Cascadable Amplifiers, pp. 445-450. The nP.Amp() objects below are matched, frequency-flat stand-ins built from the note's stated numbers. They are not measured WJ S-parameters or compression models.

Each example uses 50 ohms at 100 MHz. The input tones are 1 MHz apart. Cascade examples use -40 dBm per tone to stay in the small-signal region.

1. Noise figure: A1 followed by A5s (p. 446, Table 1)

At 15 dB first-stage gain, the table gives a 0.093 dB NF increase for A5 followed by A5 and a 0.146 dB increase for A1 followed by A5. A third A5 adds very little to the A1-first chain. The 30 ohm noise resistance below only completes the ideal A5 covariance model; it is not a measured A5 parameter.

2. A5-5 followed by PA3: output IP3 (p. 450, Fig. 26)

The worked arithmetic uses first-stage OIP3 = 20.5 dBm, PA3 gain = 14.7 dB, and PA3 OIP3 = 36 dBm. Its result is 32.6 dBm; the text mentions a 33.0 dBm measurement. The figure rounds PA3 gain to 14 dB, so this example follows the worked arithmetic.

3. Three IP3 cascade comparisons (p. 450, Figs. 27-29)

Figure 27: OIP3 values 28 and 38 dBm, second-stage gain 10 dB, combined OIP3 about 35 dBm. Figure 28: 30 and 36 dBm, gain 12 dB, result about 35 dBm. Figure 29 worked arithmetic: 19 and 35 dBm, gain 10 dB, result about 28 dBm. The Figure 29 label appears to give 17 dBm for the first stage, contrary to the worked arithmetic.

4. A9 two-tone suppression (p. 449, Fig. 23 and Table 3)

The note gives a +10 dBm fundamental and an IM3 product 52 dB below it, yielding OIP3 = +36 dBm. Table 3 lists +50 dBm second-harmonic IP2 and the text says two-tone IP2 is approximately equal. The stand-in uses 10 dB gain and 0 dBm input tones: IM3 is -42 dBm and the two-tone sum product is -30 dBm.
